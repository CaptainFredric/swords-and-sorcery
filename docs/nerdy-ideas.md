# Nerdy Ideas

Loose design notes that are worth keeping around without promoting them into the main game yet.

## Card-game versions of Spellblade and Rival

These are not final balance numbers. The point is to preserve the character-specific mechanics before they get flattened into generic "good stats + keywords" cards.

### The Rival — Legendary

**Current chassis:** 7 cost · 5 Ranged · 7 Melee · 7 Health · Armor 1 · First Strike

**Backstab** — The first enemy that attacks Rival each turn takes damage equal to Rival's Melee attack before that combat resolves.

**Interference** — The first time each turn an enemy attacks, Rival attacks a random enemy first.

**1-cost reaction, usable during the opponent's turn** — Defer Backstab from the next attack and gain Taunt until your next turn.

The idea: Rival is a better straight duelist than Spellblade and keeps interfering with the normal order of combat. He pre-empts people, punishes engagement, and can deliberately refuse a sacrificial bait attack so he can save Backstab for something more important.

### The Spellblade — Legendary

**Current chassis:** cost still being tuned · 5 Melee · 5 Ranged · 6 Health · Armor 1 · Rush

Entrance line: **"I could always do that."**

**Swords and Sorcery!** — The first time each turn an ally attacks with either combat value, Spellblade attacks the same target with the opposite combat value without receiving retaliation. "Ally" includes Spellblade himself. Bonus attacks from this ability must not recursively trigger Swords and Sorcery.

Example: if an ally makes a Melee attack, Spellblade contributes a Ranged attack; if an ally makes a Ranged attack, Spellblade contributes a Melee attack.

**I Am a Knight!** — While Spellblade has not dealt damage this turn, he has Immune.

This creates the intended tension: he begins protected, but the moment he participates — including by retaliating, attacking, or joining someone else's attack through Swords and Sorcery — he gives that protection up.

The idea: Rival interrupts other people's attacks; Spellblade enthusiastically participates in them. Rival is the stronger isolated fighter. Spellblade is the more spectacular board engine.

## Legendary-card rule of thumb

A Legendary should usually have a memorable **main thing**, not merely better stats plus evergreen keywords.

The useful test is: **if the name and art disappeared, would someone who knows the character still have a decent chance of recognizing them from how the card behaves?**

Power is not the same thing as identity. Sometimes absurd stats or keyword combinations can themselves be the Legendary violation, but usually the card should translate something specific about the character into play.
