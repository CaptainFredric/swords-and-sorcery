# Rival — Long-Term Character Direction

> Future-use design note. Not a current implementation requirement and not a commitment to make Rival playable.

## Core distinction from Spellblade

Spellblade and Rival should feel related, but not interchangeable.

**Spellblade:** reality inconveniences him, so he reinterprets reality until he remains admirable.

**Rival:** he establishes an arbitrary framework, behaves according to it, then declares himself legitimate because he obeyed his own framework.

Rival is less "Spellblade but deeper" and more a self-appointed legislator, referee, and appeals court. He genuinely respects rules. The problem is that he believes he is authorized to write them.

A useful contrast:

| Spellblade | Rival |
| --- | --- |
| Protects his **reputation** | Protects his **legitimacy** |
| Reinterprets words | Defines terms |
| "Obviously I deserve victory." | "According to the applicable conditions, I won." |
| Emotional ego | Procedural ego |
| Wants admiration | Wants recognition of authority |
| Contradiction becomes an excuse | Contradiction becomes a clause |
| Improvisational | Systematic |
| Knight who argues with reality | Knight who drafts regulations for reality |

Rival should not become only "Lawyer Knight." The procedural language is one mechanism, not his entire vocabulary. He should still have concise threats, ordinary irritation, competent combat reads, and moments where he simply sounds dangerous.

## Voice/performance direction

If Spellblade uses a slightly higher, gruff/scratchy voice, Rival should be **moderately lower but cleaner**, rather than an extreme bass duplicate.

Suggested contrast:

- lower-middle pitch;
- cleaner throat / less gravel;
- rounder chest resonance;
- more precise diction;
- more deliberate cadence;
- self-serious rather than theatrically villainous;
- sounds as though he believes he is the reasonable party.

Spellblade sounds like he continually manufactures authority. Rival sounds like he believes authority already exists because he defined it.

Do not make every line low and flat. During the chase or under pressure, let the controlled voice deteriorate. A line such as "Thank you for noticing!!" can jump upward with genuine excitement.

## Weapon direction

If Rival later becomes a standalone/playable character, keep him visibly from the same combat culture as Spellblade rather than giving him a radically unrelated weapon.

Potential fits:

- sword-and-buckler;
- narrower longsword;
- estoc / more thrust-oriented blade.

The visual/combat distinction should suggest precision, technical control, and controlling the terms of an exchange. Sword-and-buckler is especially compatible with that personality, though a narrower sword may require less animation divergence.

## Canonical sample lines / direction

Creator-proposed:

- **"By adhering to the rules I have just created, I have been following the law all along."**
- **"Your grievances are merely social constructs to be ignored."**
- **"Return if you would like to add to my tally."**

The first is close to a thesis statement for Rival: he treats authorship as jurisdiction.

The second broadens him beyond procedural jokes. He can selectively invoke sophisticated abstraction whenever it absolves him, while sounding as though the matter has now been settled.

The third is useful because Rival also needs clean, genuinely threatening lines. Not every bark should explain his philosophy.

Additional line families worth exploring:

- **"I have considered your objection. It has not survived consideration."**
- **"You were permitted to defend yourself. You did. I see no procedural issue."**
- **"There is no rule against what I have done. I checked immediately after doing it."**
- **"Your victory has been reviewed and found excessively favorable toward you."**
- **"I object."** / after being hit: **"You proceeded despite my objection!"**
- **"I recognize your right to disagree. I do not recognize your disagreement."**
- **"The terms were perfectly fair. They applied equally to everyone named Rival."**

Short systemic/combat barks:

- **"Unauthorized."** — after being hit.
- **"Objection sustained!"** — after a parry.
- **"That was outside the terms!"** — after being launched or surprised.
- **"An exception has been made."** — before using something nasty.
- **"Precedent established."** — after repeating a successful attack.
- **"Noted."** — after surviving a heavy hit.

Competent/threatening lines:

- **"You have made the same mistake twice."**
- **"I was hoping you would attempt that again."**
- **"Return if you would like to add to my tally."**

## Comedy construction

Rival's strongest jokes should often begin reasonably and reveal, late in the sentence, that he has quietly granted himself total authority.

He should not merely lie about fairness. He should be capable of producing criteria under which something is technically fair after choosing criteria that guarantee his advantage.

This keeps him distinct from Spellblade:

- Spellblade may claim the fight was fair because **he is honorable**.
- Rival may demonstrate that the fight was fair because the rules applied equally to everyone who satisfied a condition only he satisfies.

The player should occasionally hear Rival and think that his argument is coherent for half a second before noticing the jurisdiction problem.

## Relationship to Spellblade

Long-term relationship premise:

**Spellblade thinks Rival is an irritating imitation of him.**

**Rival thinks Spellblade is an unregulated version of him.**

Neither realizes why everyone else considers that distinction rather small.
