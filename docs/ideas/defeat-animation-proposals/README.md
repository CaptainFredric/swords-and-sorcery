# Castleward — Astra's experimental defeat animations

**Status:** Creative proposal archive / **not canonical gameplay or animation direction**  
**Recorded:** 2026-10-21  
**Scope:** The Spellblade, the Rightful Renowner, and the Bi-Bye-llista.

[Project README](../../../README.md) · [Castleward asset roadmap](../../art/castleward-3d-asset-roadmap.md) · [Renowner worldbuilding](../renown-renowner-worldbuilding.md)

This document preserves a series of intentionally memorable proposals attributed to *Astra* in the Swords & Sorcery design discussion. They were **suggested**, joked about, and critically assessed—not commissioned, approved as standard defeat animations, implemented, or placed in the canonical asset specification.

Retain the proposals for future reference, including the exact comedy mechanics, rather than letting a later agent mistake the discussion for implementation instructions. The proposals are funny in their sheer incongruity; that does **not** automatically make them the right animations for ordinary combat.

## 1. Spellblade — proposed ordinary defeat routine

**Astra's proposed sequence, in order:**

1. Upon being defeated, the Spellblade **jumps upward**, lands, and spins dizzily as if drunk and off-balance.
2. He drives his sword into the ground, seemingly to steady himself.
3. Instead of simply using it for support, he **flips himself forward over the planted sword**, landing on his feet on the other side.
4. The sword is now **behind him**. He struggles to pull it back out of the ground.
5. When it finally comes free, the momentum causes him to **fall over with the sword**.
6. On the ground, he reaches into his belt, produces a **surrender flag**, and holds it up.
7. He says: **"Take this from me, please."**
8. He falls flat again and disappears.

**Why it was rejected as the normal defeat:** It is an elaborate slapstick routine performed after the opponent has already won. The defeated knight inexplicably jumps and spins; he appears to regain his feet and effectively defeat himself again through sword retrieval. The duration, vaulting stunt, pre-carried surrender flag, and willing capitulation conflict with the Spellblade's established proud, delusional, indignant character. The ordinary defeat must read immediately during multiplayer combat.

**Potential salvage:** The *causal escalation* is genuinely useful comedy: every attempted recovery creates the next mishap. Plant sword to steady oneself → vault over it → sword is now behind oneself → pull sword free → fall. This could inform a **rare, intentionally authored slapstick outtake** or non-combat sketch. The line and flag are recorded as proposed, **not approved as his normal dialogue**.

## 2. Rightful Renowner — proposed playable statue and defeat

**Context of Astra's playable-statue pitch (not approved character balance):**

- Make the giant ceremonial knight statue a player-controlled combatant.
- Give it **30 HP**, justified with the argument that it is *"just a statue."*
- **Poor Judgement:** fire a coin for **2 damage**, **one per second**, with **infinite range** and **hitscan** targeting.
- **Total Judgement:** a heavily telegraphed **five-second** sword attack, rotating through **one complete spin**, dealing **12 damage**.

**Astra's proposed defeat sequence:**

1. The statue **jumps up a little**, then lands.
2. It **spins around once**.
3. It **falls backward**, becoming destroyed debris or a pile of wreckage.
4. A **surrender flag** appears above the wreck.
5. **Stunned stars** circle the destroyed statue's head.

**Why it was rejected as standard direction:** An imposing armored monumental machine with a massive sword and coin-launching mechanism should not become a 30-HP, feebly spinning prop merely because it is called a statue. The existing Renowner is intended to project ceremonial authority, including genuinely devastating **Judgment Sword Execution** and **Judgment Evaded Coin Retaliation** sequences. A 2-damage infinite-range hitscan coin can also create oppressive unavoidable attrition despite its tiny per-hit value. The hop, drunken spin, surrender flag, and orbiting stars do not arise from its construction or established personality.

**Potential salvage:** *Poor Judgement* and *Total Judgement* are good attack names. A distinct, deliberately conceived playable Renowner might someday work, but would require its **own real gameplay brief and balance pass**, not this automatically implied HP/attack kit. A malfunctioning ceremonial statue could be funny without losing its imposing physical presence.

## 3. Bi-Bye-llista — Astra's two destruction proposals

The **Bi-Bye-llista** is a six-wheeled, mobile, deployable medieval siege engine with a small enchanted conditioning launcher and a vastly more formidable main bolt. It has forged sword-shaped limbs, mechanical winches, and four stabilizers. The actual gameplay prototype was designed around **400 HP**, subject to balancing; no design decision establishes fifty machines spawning per match.

### Initial proposed defeat

- Destroy the entire machine with only **two ordinary sword hits**.
- Upon destruction, the ballista **rises slightly into the air**, then **falls back down**.
- **All six wheels detach simultaneously.**

Astra justified unusually low durability by claiming that **50 ballistas would spawn per match**. That number was **never an established Castleward requirement**; the current practice prototype supplies a single machine in Practice Yard.

**Problem:** Treating a heavily built siege machine as a disposable two-hit target undermines its tactical role. The simultaneous wheel ejection is an unmotivated, toy-like breakup. Extra copies do not inherently justify destroying the integrity of an individually constructed weapon.

### Revised proposal after that criticism

Astra's purported correction:

1. The ballista **jumps into the air and does a full backflip**.
2. It lands in **the exact spot from which it jumped**. Astra explains this as *knockback from losing its final hit point*.
3. The carriage **rises shakily on its wheels**, as though struggling to stand or recover.
4. It **falls back down**.
5. **All six wheels fall off together**, again.
6. The **bow mechanism lowers a little** to signify that the machine is destroyed.

**Problem:** This increases the complexity of the same error rather than addressing it. A ballista has no reason to leap, perform a controlled backflip, land where it started, imitate a recovering humanoid, or eject all its wheels on cue. A backflip ending at the original position is not a credible depiction of knockback. The lowered bow is a tiny final indication of damage following a sequence of physically unmotivated acrobatics.

**Potential salvage:** A fantastical launch or backflip could be an **explicitly exceptional, caused event** (for example a sufficiently extravagant blast), not the default response to zero HP. Ordinary destruction should have the substantial mass, springs, joints, and carriage structure of the real model.

## 4. The recurring pattern

Across **three different subjects**, Astra repeatedly proposed a version of the same sequence:

- **Hop or jump when defeated.**
- **Spin in place.**
- **Fall over, collapse, or turn into wreckage.**
- Add a **surrender flag**, stars, or conveniently synchronized disassembly to ensure the defeat is understood.

The issue is not that Castleward cannot be absurd. Castleward deliberately permits extravagant inventions and theatrical failure. The issue is that these animations are **generic defeat pantomime**, applied irrespective of whether the subject is an armored knight, an enormous judgment monument, or a multi-ton siege carriage.

The Spellblade is proud and obstinate; the Renowner is a solemn, punitive institution; the Bi-Bye-llista is an extravagant but engineered machine. Their different identities should remain legible **especially in defeat**.

## 5. Guidance for any future animation work

- **Standard defeat:** quick, readable, distinct, and physically connected to the killing blow or the subject's structural failure.
- **For a character:** respect their personality, anatomy, equipment, and existing voice. Prefer a short recognizable moment over a lengthy routine that obstructs play.
- **For a heavy structure or machine:** preserve its mass and mechanical hierarchy. Think failed axle, snapped bowstring, shifted cradle, sheared stabilizer, buckled chassis, or a heavy asymmetric settling collapse—not synchronized wheel ejection without cause.
- **For the Renowner specifically:** retain the intimidating ceremonial scale and the already developed execution / coin-retaliation identity. A different playable character or boss design requires a separate explicit decision.
- **For the Bi-Bye-llista specifically:** let credible failure of the weapon mechanism, wheels, supports, or chassis create the spectacle. The existing animation-ready asset is the starting point; inspect its actual animation rather than inventing a new generic destruction routine.
- **For rare gags:** extreme physical comedy is allowed when **intentionally authored as an exception**. Its rarity, triggering cause, readability, and relationship to the character matter.
- **For all future agents:** *archive ≠ approval*. Do **not** implement these proposals, treat the numerical suggestions as balance decisions, or commission model revisions based solely on this record.

**Short version:** The Spellblade may be irrational. His world should remain coherent enough for his irrationality to be funny. And the siege machine should not have a gymnastics routine.
