# Castleward worldbuilding — Renown, the Rightful Renowner and judgment gags

**Status:** Creative/worldbuilding concepts and proposed future animated scenes; **not implemented gameplay, confirmed audio, or shipped 3D assets**. The concept sheet for the Rightful Renowner is a visual reference, not a mechanically verified Blender model.

[Project README](../../README.md) · [3D asset roadmap](../art/castleward-3d-asset-roadmap.md)

## Creative principle

Major Castleward concepts should be **just a little insane, in exactly the right way**. Start with something recognizable, give it a slightly preposterous but internally comprehensible premise, then treat that premise with total sincerity. Not every ordinary prop or line must be a gag. Resist overexplaining the absurdity or automatically translating every joke into a game feature.

## Renown: prestige that one possesses

**Working worldbuilding idea, in the creator's words:**

> Spellblades like to hoard Renown. They don't really trade it away. But the more you have, the more Renown you have...

The circular statement is the point. The Spellblades' fundamental motive is not efficient exchange or practical purchasing power: **the possession of Renown is its own justification**. They accumulate it because having more of it means having more of it, which they regard as plainly desirable. To ask what they intend to do with it rather misses the point.

A Spellblade might count, display, guard, boast about, and obsessively collect Renown with no interest in spending it; relinquishing it would mean having less Renown. The attitude can be communal, competitive, self-important and strangely obvious to those within that culture.

The contradiction between grand institutions for conferring honor and the possessive triviality of actually collecting it is fertile ground for comedy. The setting need not announce a critique or explain why it is silly.

### Death, wills and ownership

Some fallen Spellblades **insist on keeping their Renown to themselves even after death**, explicitly specifying this in their wills. Their passing is apparently no reason to relinquish something they have accumulated.

Possible scene: a solemn reading of a knight's will leaves his sword to one heir, his armor to another, and his Renown **to himself**. The solicitor treats the arrangement with appropriate gravity. It need not be explained how the deceased intends to use it.

### Theft and the arithmetic of honor

**Creator's rule:** “Stealing Renown is acceptable, if you end up with more.”

This is deliberately self-serving moral reasoning, not a principled exception to a well-defined theft law. Success at retaining more Renown supplies its own justification. The same knight may demand permanent ownership of his own Renown while regarding somebody else's collection as an attractive acquisition.

A Spellblade might scold a thief for ending up with *less* Renown, rather than for stealing. Avoid resolving that contradiction through a detailed legal code or economic simulation: the circular logic and absolute sincerity are the worldbuilding.

Neither premise requires changes to the existing progression wallet or a new player-versus-player stealing feature. They can stand as character beliefs and sources of scenes.

**Distinction:** This is an *in-fiction cultural disposition*, not a request to rewrite the game's presently implemented, spendable Renown balance and cosmetic unlocks. If a physical Renown coin is depicted in a scene, that does not by itself establish a full currency or trading system.

## The Rightful Renowner

**Proposed monument / future authored 3D model:** a grand low-poly statue of an armored knight, standing on a stone and brass ceremonial pedestal. Red trident heraldry and a crimson plume complement cyan-lit mechanisms, with an articulated two-handed sword and a **rather modest dispensing aperture/tray** at the base.

The statue is treated as an entirely legitimate authority on who merits Renown. Its sword rises and dips in automated knighting motions before the dispenser produces a small reward. It is not a comic clown or an obviously possessed evil statue: its cold, stately seriousness makes the outcomes funny.

**Physical animation premise:** articulated shoulders, elbows, wrists and sword; a reachable ceremonial position; occasional downstroke sufficient to strike someone on the dais; a distinct little mechanical reward dispenser. Preserve the imposing sculpture-versus-tiny-tray contrast. The source model, animation feasibility and integration have not yet been verified.

### Scene A — The judgment is fatal

*Future gag / animation concept.*

A knight approaches to be honored by the Rightful Renowner. He takes the ceremonial position and bows while the statue raises its enormous sword.

For a moment everything resembles the normal knighting ritual.

The statue brings its sword down in earnest and **slays the knight**.

It then calmly returns the sword to its resting position.

A small Renown coin slides out of its little tray.

*Clink.*

The knight is in no condition to collect it.

**Comic structure:** the procedure carries on with solemn institutional consistency. A ceremony meant to recognize worthiness instead executes its applicant, and the dispensing mechanism completes its routine regardless. The statue gives no villainous laugh or explanation. This is an occasional judgment outcome, not a mandate for random unavoidable player deaths.

### Scene B — Spellblade surpasses judgment, but not compensation

*Future gag / animation concept. Preserve the creator's spoken lines as written.*

The Rightful Renowner raises its enormous sword and attempts to execute Spellblade.

Spellblade simply **steps outside the sword's striking reach**. The blade crashes into the ceremonial platform, missing him.

Spellblade laughs, delighted to have beaten the monument at its own business:

> **Spellblade:** "AHAHAHA! I have surpassed judgement"

The statue withdraws its sword and returns to its formal resting pose.

Spellblade bows to it, apparently magnanimous in victory:

> **Spellblade:** "I shall receive your compensation"

A coin is dispensed at absurdly high speed and **strikes Spellblade hard enough to knock him unconscious**.

The monument remains impassive.

**Comic structure:** the huge, telegraphed weapon fails; the tiny, apparently harmless reward dispenser succeeds. Spellblade's decision to bow and collect his due creates the reversal. A clear staging option is for the bow to put his helmet directly into the coin's path. Do not have the statue overtly reveal a second attack or spell out the punchline; it has simply dispensed Renown.

## Future animation / direction notes

- The **normal knighting motion** and the **fatal sword downstroke** should begin from the same solemn ceremonial behavior. The execution lands as a reversal, not as a character suddenly becoming evil.
- In the Spellblade variant, make his **successful sidestep unmistakable** and allow enough room for the smug declaration, the sword's unthreatening reset, and his formal bow before the coin's almost instantaneous impact.
- The dispensing aperture should be readable but modest relative to the monumental pedestal; its shocking projectile speed is a joke about the **same** little apparatus, not a newly revealed turret.
- Use the physicality of the monument, pauses and impacts to deliver the comedy; dialogue belongs principally to Spellblade.
- Future implementation is optional. These notes are creative continuity for art, animation, writing and worldbuilding, **not acceptance criteria or required modifications to the live Renown system**.

## Future herald concept — Strike While Hot!

**Status:** Proposed herald/achievement concept; artwork and unlock condition are not implemented or finalized.

- **Title:** **Strike While Hot!**
- **Flavor text (preserve exact wording):** “THIS was now planned!”
- **Art concept:** A sword has been **thrown spinning through a fireball**, cleaving the spell cleanly into **two separating halves** while the sword continues through the cut. The motion is a precise, extravagant sword-versus-sorcery feat rather than an ordinary sword pose or fireball icon.
- **Composition:** Make the single thrown sword's trajectory and spin readable, with a distinct separation through the fireball. Keep movement accents restrained enough that both severed halves and the weapon remain instantly identifiable at herald size. Preserve Castleward's bold, stylized heraldic treatment.
- **Character/comedy:** The Spellblade retroactively declares an implausibly successful improvisation to have been planned. The intentionally contradictory tense in **“THIS was now planned!”** is the punchline, not a typo to correct.

The title and flavor text are established creative choices. Do not infer a gameplay unlock trigger or award this herald until its actual condition is separately designed and implemented.
