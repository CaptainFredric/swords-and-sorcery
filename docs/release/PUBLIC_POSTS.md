## LinkedIn

I’ve been building Swords & Sorcery, a free first person multiplayer game you can play in your browser. Share a room code and duel a friend, or start with bots and practice.

Play: https://captainfredric.github.io/swords-and-sorcery/

The latest addition is Greener Pastures, a voiced tutorial that teaches movement, swordplay and sorcery through an increasingly unreasonable feud with another knight. An introduction eventually becomes a chase.

The Spellblade’s response to discovering his stamina bar:

“Why is it going down? I did not order it to.”

I designed the combat rules, character, abilities and dialogue, and perform the Spellblade’s voice. AI has helped with implementation, networking, debugging, testing and Blender tooling. Getting the result to feel like the game I intended has meant a great deal of playing, questioning and revising.

One important technical change: solo combat now runs locally in the browser, while multiplayer keeps the server in charge of combat. Both use the same rules.

I’m continuing to refine the character, tutorial pacing and combat presentation. If you try it, I’d appreciate feedback on the first few minutes, how the controls feel, and whether a duel makes sense without me explaining it.

## Handshake

My entry for the Handshake AI Skills Studio × OpenAI Multiplayer Game Challenge is Swords & Sorcery: a free browser game about sword combat, sorcery and a knight with a flexible understanding of honor.

Play with a friend through a room code, find an open match, or try the solo modes:
https://captainfredric.github.io/swords-and-sorcery/

The most distinctive addition is Greener Pastures. Its voiced tutorial teaches through an argument with a rival knight, then turns that argument into combat and pursuit. The jokes are authored; the sword contacts and stamina are real.

I created the design, character direction, dialogue and Spellblade recordings. AI helped implement and debug the game, networking and Blender workflows. One lesson has been learning to preserve consistent combat rules while making local solo play responsive.

Try a duel or the tutorial and tell me where you first felt confused, or where something worked particularly well. Both are useful.
